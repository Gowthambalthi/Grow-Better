# GB01 / GB02 / GB03 — backtested on live data, 2026-10-18

This is the second pass on the three GB systems. The first pass audited a pasted
report and found one fabricated improvement. This one does what the request said:
**test all three on data the repository did not contain, and fix what is actually
broken.**

Nothing below is measured on synthetic data. Every bar is either the recorded
1-minute corpus or a bar fetched from the broker on 2026-10-18.

## 0. What "live data" means in this document

The recorded corpus ends **2026-10-06**. So that gap was closed first:

session-close exit, entry window minus the blocked windows, cost charged in R.

## 2. Bugs found and fixed

### B1 — the shipped OF03 cutoff was pinned for a reason that cannot exist

`strategy-core/engines.js` carried:

```js
config: { maxSignalTime: 1100, /* 11:00 IST … (57.9% win rate, netPF 2.19) */ }
```

The 57.9% / 2.19 came from an EMA20 filter that admits **zero** of ten OF03 longs
(previous pass, §2 of the edge review). The registry is what the live engine
reads and what the backtest harness runs, while the engine's own `DEFAULTS`, its
header and `docs/ORDERFLOW-PHASE2.md` all say **12:30**. So live and backtest
agreed with each other and disagreed with the documentation, on the strength of a
number that could not have been measured.

Re-measured on the canonical corpus, both cutoffs in one run
([gb_of_cutoff_canonical.js](../scratch/gb_of_cutoff_canonical.js)):

| cutoff | trades | win | netPF | net R | maxDD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11:00 | 21 | 52.4% | 1.746 | +9.24R | 3.76R |
| **12:30** | **25** | 48.0% | **1.997** | **+15.97R** | 4.91R |

12:30 gives up 4.4 points of win rate and earns **+6.7R**. The eight live
sessions pointed the same way (11:00 → 5 trades/+5.0R; 12:30 → 8/+15.5R). The
registry now pins `1230`, the fabricated comment is replaced with the measurement,
and `data/terminal/backtest_results.json` was regenerated with all four engines so
the board quotes the configuration that ships.

### B2 — the catalogue described a different engine than the one running

| label said | the code does |
|---|---|
| GB01: "14:00 IST entry cutoff" | `maxEntryMinute = 14*60+45` → **14:45** |
| GB03a: "09:25–11:00 … window" | no lower bound at all (harness window opens 09:20); the cutoff was 11:00, now 12:30 |
| GB03a: "with trend alignment" | no trend gate exists — the setup arms on the far side of every EMA by construction |

All three are now **derived from the constants** (`hhmm` / `hhmmOfClock` over the
engine's own DEFAULTS), and `test/registryParity.test.js` fails if a label and the
engine it describes ever separate again. GB02's description now also states that it
shares the 5m engine's gates and is nearly always refused before a trade exists.

### B3 — `age < 1`: the right rule with the wrong reason

The FVG engine registers a zone on the first 1-minute bar **after** its candle
closed (the gap candle ends 09:49; the engine sees the zone at the 09:50 bar), then
refused that bar with `if (age < 1) continue; // its own bar cannot retest it`.
The 09:50 bar is not the candle's own bar — it is the first bar that could legally
trade the zone.

So the value was defensible and the justification was wrong. Measured on the
canonical corpus ([gb_fvg_age_ab.js](../scratch/gb_fvg_age_ab.js)):

| | trades | netPF | net R |
|---|---|---|---|
| `minZoneAgeBars: 1` (shipped) | 73 | **1.940** | **+43.6R** |
| `minZoneAgeBars: 0` (the "fix") | 70 | 1.245 | +11.9R |

Trading the immediate bar is worse: it touches the zone while the move is still
travelling and, under the one-shot rule, that weak touch consumes the zone. The
deferral is now an explicit, measured constant instead of an off-by-one explained
by a false premise.

### B4 — the gate that actually decides GB01 was inherited by accident

This is the find that reframes the whole system. On 20 symbols, the planner's
extension gate refused **4,032 FVG retests** — and **4,031 of them (99.98%) for one
reason**: `ATR % too quiet to reach a target`. The threshold is
`tradePlanner.DEFAULTS.minAtrPct = 0.60%`, a floor calibrated for an engine that
entered late; on 1-minute bars the decision-bar ATR% is median **0.14%**, p90
**0.27%**, and **0.0%** of decision bars reach 0.60%. `strategies/orderFlow.js` had
already re-calibrated this to 0.10% with a comment saying so. FVG never did — it
simply inherited the number.

Loosening it, measured on corpus A:

| floor | trades | netPF | net R | expectancy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0.60 (shipped)** | **28** | **1.65** | **+11.5R** | +0.409R |
| 0.30 | 132 | 1.01 | +1.3R | +0.010R |
| 0.15 | 367 | 0.86 | −41.9R | −0.114R |
| 0.10 | 404 | 0.87 | −42.4R | −0.105R |

**The floor is the strategy.** Every larger sample of the same retest rule is
negative, and the positive measurement is a 28-trade slice of extreme-volatility
minutes. That is now written in `strategies/fvg/index.js` next to the constant
instead of being left for someone to rediscover, and the catalogue says it too.
The rule was **not** loosened to manufacture trades — that would have turned a thin
positive into a large negative.

### B5 — the pins followed the change

`orderFlow.js`'s header now states the 12:30 measurement instead of the 18-trade
2.340 line; the frozen copy was re-copied; `docs/ORDERFLOW-PHASE2.md` and
`backtest/phase5Preflight.js` record the new hash (`42e48fcf2488ce08`), and
`test/ofEngine.test.js` pins the cutoff by **object identity** now, because
comparing values stopped proving anything once the registry and the engine agreed.

## 3. What each system is, after the fixes

**GB01 — real, but far thinner than the label suggests.** 69 trades in 705 sessions,
netPF 1.87, both halves and both sides positive, and +5.5R on the live window. It
is also, measurably, a *volatility-regime* filter wearing an FVG's clothes: take
retests in ordinary minutes and the same rule loses 42R. Treat it as one or two
calls per month, sized for a 42% win rate and long losing runs, not as a daily
engine.

**GB02 — does not exist in practice.** 4 trades in 705 sessions, 1 in the last 83,
0 on the live window. Nothing in the measurement justifies a rule change; the
honest action is to stop presenting it beside GB01 as if the two were additive.

**GB03a — shorts, and only shorts.** At 12:30 it is 25 trades / netPF 2.00, but the
13 shorts carry +19.3R and the 12 longs lose −3.3R. The 12:30 change added trades
and profit; it did not fix the long side, and a filter fitted to twelve longs is
exactly how the 2.19 number was invented in the first place. What this needs is
more trades — a wider universe or a longer window — not another gate.

* GB02's 4 trades cannot support any conclusion in either direction.

## 6. The live connection (added after the backtest pass)

Until this section was written, **GB01 and GB02 had no live driver at all.** The intraday
tier's "FVG" row was `Sweep + FVG`, a different rule that happens to read gaps — so the
strategy with 73 measured trades had no row on the board, and the row that existed had no
measurement behind it. GB03a was live, but it was being fed timestamps that broke its session.

### 6.1 What was added

| piece | file | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| FVG live adapter | [strategies/fvg/liveAdapter.js](../strategies/fvg/liveAdapter.js) | drives the measured engine from `symBars`: closed minutes only, each fed once, stale answers dropped |
| scan wiring | [strategies/orderflow/liveMinuteEngine.js](../strategies/orderflow/liveMinuteEngine.js) | turns an FVG signal into a call with the engine's own stop/T1 and the live LTP as entry |
| row identity | [common/market/callBookHistory.js](../common/market/callBookHistory.js) | a writer-stamped `strategyId` names the row: `FG01`, `FG02`, `ORDERFLOW` |
| tier-2 label | [public/final.html](../public/final.html) | the INTRADAY header now names GB01 / GB02 / GB03a instead of "2 live engines" |
| tests | [test/fvgLiveWiring.test.js](../test/fvgLiveWiring.test.js) | 7 tests: closed-bar rule, once-only feed, staleness, plan geometry, row naming, and a scan that must put an FG01 row on the book |

### 6.2 The live bug this exposed

The live path formatted bar times as **`HH:MM:SS`** (`getISTTimeString`). The engines group
sessions by `String(time).slice(0, 10)` — on `HH:MM:SS` that slice is the **clock**, so every
bar opened a new "session". For the live OF03 that meant: VWAP and the day's high/low were
computed from **one bar**, `rangePosPct` was a position inside that single bar, targets could
be drawn from a one-bar "day high", the per-day signal cap reset every minute, and the
cooldown never survived a single bar. The backtest never had this problem, so the live engine
did not run the configuration that was measured.

`istBarTime()` now emits the corpus format (`YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`), and a bar with no clock is
refused by name (`clocked`) instead of being stamped with `Date.now()`.

### 6.3 Proof, on real sessions

[gb_live_dryrun.js](../scratch/gb_live_dryrun.js) drives `scanMinuteTape()` — the function the
server runs every minute — over the eight broker-fetched sessions, minute by minute, and prints
the calls the live book actually creates.

```
2026-10-14 09:51 | FG01 | GB01 — 5m FVG Retest | INTRADAY | AEROENTER BUY @ 138.95 SL 137.99 T1 140.17
```

That is a row the TIER 2 table paints: `strategyId` FG01, tier INTRADAY, the engine's own stop
and target, entry at the live price. It is produced by the same code the server runs, from real
recorded bars — not by the backtest harness.

### 6.4 What Monday will show

* **TIER 2 (INTRADAY)** gets one row per GB system that fires — `GB01 — 5m FVG Retest`,
  `GB02 — 15m FVG Retest`, `GB03a — OF03 Exhaustion Reversal` — each with live R, P&L and the

* **GB02 may never appear.** Four trades in 705 sessions is its record; if a row shows up, treat

### 6.6 GB01 now reports its own work — the tier-2 row can no longer be silent about why

The measured FVG engine is on the live tape, but until now **nothing counted what it did**. The
adapter discards what it will not arm (`push` returns null for a refusal, for a stale answer and
for a minute that has not closed), so the live board answered "no calls" identically for three
different situations: no gap formed, a gap formed and the gate refused it, and the adapter never
saw a closed bar at all. On Monday that is the difference between "quiet" and "broken".

**What changed**

| where | what it does |
|---|---|
| [liveAdapter.js](strategies/fvg/liveAdapter.js) | every branch that throws an answer away increments a counter: `refused[GATE]` by the engine's own gate name, `staleDropped`, `noClosedBar`, `noSignal`, `barsFed`, `dayResets`, plus `lastSignal`. `stats()` returns a copy; `resetStats()` zeroes the telemetry without dropping engine state. |
| [liveMinuteEngine.js](strategies/orderflow/liveMinuteEngine.js) | the scan publishes them: `scanFunnel.fvg = { symbolsTracked, fedThisScan, barsFedThisScan, signalsThisScan, refusedThisScan, sinceBoot: {...}, note }`. The cumulative counters survive the per-scan funnel reset (read as a delta against a snapshot taken when the scan starts), so `sinceBoot.barsFed` is the day's evidence and `barsFedThisScan` is this minute's work. |
| the live call note | now quotes the **authorized window** (33 trades, netPF 2.36, +0.812R per trade) instead of the whole-corpus framing, and states the base rate in the call itself. |

**Reproduced on real tape** — 60 symbols × the eight broker sessions of 2026-10-07…16, replayed
through `scanMinuteTape()`:

```
=== GB01 TELEMETRY (the funnel the board publishes) ===
symbols tracked 60 · bars fed this scan 54 · signals this scan 0
since boot: barsFed 142221 · signals 1 · staleDropped 0 · dayResets 0 · refused {"EXTENSION":478,"RR":1,"STOP_TOO_WIDE":1}
last signal: {"symbol":"AEROENTER","setup":"FG01","side":"LONG","barTimeIST":"2026-10-14 09:50"}
```

142,221 closed bars fed, exactly one call — and **the refusal histogram says which gate is doing
the silencing: EXTENSION 478, against RR 1 and STOP_TOO_WIDE 1**. One caveat on reading it: the
planner stops at the FIRST gate that refuses, so a count of 478 at EXTENSION means 478 decisions
died at the extension check and never reached the ATR floor behind it. The histogram ranks gates
in the order they run, not by how much each would cost in isolation.

**Monday, read `scanFunnel.fvg`:** `sinceBoot.barsFed > 0` means the measured engine is being fed
(the row is then empty by measurement), and `sinceBoot.barsFed === 0` after the first scan means
the wiring or the tape is the fault, not the strategy. `refused` names the gate responsible.

**Tests:** [fvgLiveWiring.test.js](test/fvgLiveWiring.test.js) is 12/12 — the four added cases pin
that a refusal is named, that staleness is counted as staleness rather than as a refusal, that an
armed signal is stamped with the bar that produced it, and that a second scan in the same minute
reports `barsFedThisScan: 0` while the cumulative figure stands.

### 6.7 The live feed was 136 names wide, on a board that approves 2,017

GB01's tier-2 row can only fill from names the live capture subscribes to, so the capture's scope is
part of the wiring, not a research detail. It read `board.rows` — **the rows the board DISPLAYS**,
which on 2026-10-18 was three symbols — plus `backtest/f1/universe.json`: **136 names in total**,
while its own comment said "the board's own OK universe" and `board.json` reported `notBlocked:
2017`. At GB01's measured 1 call per 1,022 symbol-days that difference is a week of empty rows.

**What changed**

| where | what it does |
|---|---|
| [buildBoard.js](scripts/buildBoard.js) | publishes the OK universe as a **LIST**, not only a counter: `universe.okSymbols` (capped by `GB_OK_SYMBOLS_CAP`, default 900) plus `universe.okSymbolsSource` carrying the counts and the reason. |
| [armCapture.js](strategies/orderflow/armCapture.js) | `chooseCaptureSymbols({ board, extras, limit })` — a pure, testable decision: `board.universe.okSymbols` first, then the legacy `board.rows` shape, then the f1 extras; deduped, `-EQ` stripped, capped. |
| [Server.js](Server.js) | uses it and logs `universe: source board.universe.okSymbols · armed 500 of 907 available (okList 900 · rows 3 · extras 134 · cap 500 · dropped 407)`. |

**Which names, and why not `tracked − blocked`:** the 2,934-symbol pool is a *count* from
`data/universe_filtered.json`, and that file lists only what it **passed** (1,006 names) — the other
1,927 are rejected for price, volume or liquidity and are not tradeable by any rule that reads the
tape. So the list is *passed minus the blocks in force*. The old counter is still carried as
`okSymbolsSource.poolNotBlocked` so the two numbers sit side by side instead of one silently
replacing the other.

**Verified in the running server** (`PORT=4300`, capture armed by default):

```
[orderflow/capture] universe: source board.universe.okSymbols · armed 500 of 907 available (okList 900 · rows 3 · extras 134 · cap 500 · dropped 407)
[orderflow/capture] armed on 499 tokens (1 symbol(s) had no token: HEG) · mode QUOTE · correlation gb_capture
```

136 names → **499 tokens**, and every line of that widening is stated. Expected effect on GB01 by the
measured rate: ~0.13 → **~0.49 calls/day** (roughly one call every two sessions instead of every
seven), and the number is logged rather than assumed. `GB_TICK_CAPTURE_LIMIT` sets the ceiling
(0 = no cap, which would arm all 907); `GB_OK_SYMBOLS_CAP` sets how many the board publishes.

**One documentation lie repaired in passing.** The capture's header claimed it "does not run at all
unless `env.research.tickCapture()` is true, which is false for every value except the literal '1'".
The call site arms it when `GB_TICK_CAPTURE !== '0'` — i.e. **by default** — and nothing in the
function reads that flag. Believing the comment would have meant arriving on Monday with no live bars
at all, because this capture is the only writer of the ticks `liveMinuteEngine.symBars` is built
from.

**Tests:** [captureUniverse.test.js](test/captureUniverse.test.js) — 5 cases pinning the source
order, the legacy fallback, blocked-row exclusion, `-EQ` normalisation, the cap and its reported
drop, that an explicit `0` means *no cap* rather than "use the default", and that an empty board
yields nothing rather than an invented list.

**What this costs on disk — measured, not projected.** Friday's tape
(`data/orderflow/ticks-2026-10-16.jsonl`) holds 3,121,464 rows across **557 distinct symbols** in
374MB, i.e. **688KB per symbol-session**. At the new 500-name cap that is **~0.33GB per session
(~7GB/month)**; at 907 names, ~0.6GB. So the widening restores roughly the volume the capture was
already writing on Friday — worth knowing that today's 136 was itself a **regression** from 557,
caused by `board.rows` shrinking to the three displayed rows, not a deliberate narrowing.
`GB_TICK_CAPTURE_LIMIT` is the dial (`200` → ~0.13GB/session) and `GB_TICK_CAPTURE=0` turns recording
off without touching the live strategies' configuration.

## 8. GB03a on the phone, and the 3-second gap between messages (2026-10-18, third pass)

### 8.1 The claim checked: "GB03 is positive on the short call"

It is — **on the short side only**, and that distinction was missing from the live path.

| slice | side | trades | net R | per trade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 705-session corpus (12:30 pin) | **SHORT** | 13 | **+19.27R** | **+1.48R** |
| 705-session corpus | LONG | 12 | −3.29R | −0.27R |
| authorized 172-session window | **SHORT** | 4 | −0.054R | **−0.014R (breakeven)** |
| authorized 172-session window | LONG | 5 | −3.75R | −0.750R |

Source: `docs/GB-LIVE-2026-10-18.md` §4 and `scratch/gb_learning_window.json`
(`engines.ORDERFLOW.long` / `.short`, both cost cases). So the other agent's claim is
correct as stated about the corpus, and incomplete in one respect worth stating: inside the
pre-committed window the short side is **breakeven, not positive** (4 trades, −0.014R), while
the long side is clearly negative in both slices. The better-measured side of an unproven
setup, not a proven edge.

The live scan armed **both** sides of GB03a while the setup was being described as positive.
That is fixed at the live boundary: `GB03_SIDES=short` (default) arms the measured side,
counts the other in the funnel (`scanFunnel.gb03.sideRefused`) instead of dropping it in
silence, and the row/alert says which side it is and why. `GB03_SIDES=both|long` restores the
old behaviour without a code edit.

### 8.2 The alert said BUY no matter which way the call pointed

`dispatchTelegramNewCall` was hard-coded `NEW INSTITUTIONAL BUY CALL`, and the log line printed
`BUY` for every call. A GB03a SHORT — the side with the only positive measurement — reached the
phone announced as a buy. The message is now built from the call's own side and its GB code:

```
🔻 NEW SHORT CALL · GB03a · ACMESOLAR
🔻 SELL / SHORT @ live LTP ₹439.65 · INTRADAY
🛑 Stop above ₹440.31 (+0.15% of entry)
🎯 Target 1 ₹437.99 (+0.38%)
⚖️ Risk : Reward 1 : 2.51 · risk ₹0.66/share
⚙️ GB03a — OF03 Exhaustion Reversal
📋 <the engine's own reason + the measured side, with the window caveat>
🕒 Armed 09:48:00 IST · the entry is the live LTP, not a re-quote
```

The row on `/final` and the message on the phone now carry the **same sentence**
(`gb03Note()`), so a call cannot be presented one way on the page and another way on the phone.

### 8.3 One message at a time, three seconds apart

Several calls can be armed by the same scan, and they were sent concurrently — one burst, and
under Telegram's throttle some never arrived. The pace lives in the **sender**
(`common/notifications/sendPacer.js`, wired into `telegramService.sendPhoneNotification`), so
every caller inherits it: new calls, exits and the 15:30 scorecard. The gap is measured from
the END of the previous message (a slow send does not stack), a failed send releases the queue
instead of wedging every later alert, and it is the dial `TELEGRAM_GAP_MS` (default **3000**;
`0` disables). Pinned by `test/telegramCalls.test.js`, including one test that drives the real
sender with only the transport stubbed and asserts the two messages leave ≥300ms apart
(the arithmetic of 3000 is covered separately with a fake clock).

### 8.4 Where the 3-4 calls a day comes from (and why 500 names will not do it)

The rate is per symbol-day, not per universe: GB01 ≈1 call per 1,022 symbol-days, GB03a ≈1 per
2,820. Measured again on real broker tape in `scratch/gb_240.log` (240 symbols × 8 sessions):
2 calls, 566,495 bars fed — 1 per 960 symbol-days, consistent.

| armed universe | GB01/day | GB03a/day | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 136 (the old capture) | 0.13 | 0.05 | ~0.18 |
| 500 (today's cap) | 0.49 | 0.18 | **~0.67** |
| 2,017 (the board's OK universe, `GB_TICK_CAPTURE_LIMIT=0`) | 1.97 | 0.72 | **~2.7** |

So ~3 calls/day needs the **full ~2,000-name universe**, not 500 — and at the measured tape
cost that is ~1.3GB/session rather than ~0.33GB. The dial is `GB_TICK_CAPTURE_LIMIT`; `0`
means no cap. The tier-2 empty state now states this mechanism, so a quiet 500-name Monday is
not read as a dead feed.

### 8.5 Tier 2 of /final — what actually changed

GB03a already reached tier 2 (it declares the INTRADAY tier and `callBookHistory.strategyOf`
